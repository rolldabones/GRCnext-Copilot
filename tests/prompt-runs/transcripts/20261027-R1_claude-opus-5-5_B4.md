## Short answer

I cannot mark the Exercises domain as L3 Exercised on this basis. A tabletop is a **T1 facilitated tabletop**, and the scoring method caps T0 and T1 exercises below L3. In a tabletop, participants discuss actions rather than perform them, so it cannot show that a switch or exit runs within tolerance. The only exception is a capability that genuinely could not be executed in a controlled test, and that case would need a documented justification.

There is also an evidence problem. "It went well" is an unverified statement. No exercise record has been supplied, so any credit is provisional.

## ⧉ Largest gap

There is no evidence that any switch or exit has been performed against a running clock. A successful discussion can hide the delays that cause real tolerance failures, such as an unavailable decision-maker, expired access or a slow vendor. Until an exercise measures elapsed time from onset to stabilization, the organization holds documented options, not demonstrated ones.

## Path to L3

1. **Choose the critical service and its approved impact tolerance.** This is the target the exercise is measured against.
2. **Run a T2 controlled test or a T3 end-to-end rehearsal.** Actually invoke at least one material switch or exit.
3. **Time every phase of the execution clock,** starting at onset: Detect, Interpret, Decide, Authorize, Execute, Verify, Communicate, Stabilize.
4. **Record which actions were performed and which were simulated.**
5. **Capture findings as corrective actions** with owners and target dates, and plan a retest.

L3 then applies only to the path that was actually exercised.
