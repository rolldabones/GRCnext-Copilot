## Why "probably fine" does not score higher

Under the GRCnext™ maturity scale:

- **L2 Designed** requires documented triggers, delegated decision rights, prerequisites and rollback. I have not seen any of these.
- **L3 Exercised** requires the switch to have been invoked under a severe but plausible scenario, with timing captured. A tabletop (T1) does not qualify.
- **L4 Instrumented** requires monitoring, current metrics and closed corrective-action loops.

Runbooks, even good ones, cap at L2 until someone has actually executed them and the result was recorded.

## Stress-test questions the runbooks must survive

For each material payments switch (for example reroute to a secondary processor, pause outbound payments, invoke manual payment processing, isolate a compromised integration or fail over to a recovery region):

1. **Trigger.** What observable condition invokes it, and who detects it?
2. **Authority.** Who may authorize it, and who is the named deputy outside business hours?
3. **Access.** Do the operators hold current credentials, tokens and tool access today?
4. **Downstream effects.** What happens to in-flight transactions, duplicates, reconciliation and settlement cutoffs?
5. **Communications.** Who tells customers, the processor or scheme and any regulator, and how quickly?
6. **Rollback.** How do you return to normal service without double-processing or losing transactions?
7. **Timing.** How long does the full clock take, from onset through Stabilize, against your impact tolerance?
8. **Evidence.** What record does execution produce?
